# School Calendars and Morning Air Quality in the Monterrey Metropolitan Area

Analysis code and results for the article *School Calendars and Morning Air Quality in Monterrey, Mexico: A Within-Day Analysis of the School-Arrival Window* (Flores Uribe, Gómez Muñoz and Ruiz Hernández, ACESD 2026). The version corresponding to the article is **v1.0.0**.

Summary
-------
School calendars synchronize the mobility of hundreds of thousands of households into a short daily window, but measuring their contribution to urban air pollution is hard: school terms follow the seasons, and daily pollution series are strongly autocorrelated, so simple class-versus-vacation comparisons are unreliable. This repository quantifies the association between the official school calendar and morning pollutant concentrations in the Monterrey Metropolitan Area using hourly CO, NO2, O3, PM10 and PM2.5 from the complete official SIMA network (fifteen stations, 2023–2025). The core design is a within-day double difference: the 07:00–09:00 school-arrival window is contrasted with a same-day 10:00–12:00 control window, and the resulting gap is regressed on the official school-term indicator with zone, month and day-of-week fixed effects and errors clustered by ISO week. Wild cluster bootstrap, equivalence tests and Romano–Wolf corrections complete the primary inference; day-level contrasts, station-level estimates, hourly profiles and leave-one-zone-out analyses serve as diagnostics and sensitivity checks; and everything is run under two independent imputations.

Analytical hierarchy
--------------------
The primary analysis is the conceptually motivated within-day double difference comparing the 07:00–09:00 school-arrival window with the same-day 10:00–12:00 control window, with weekly-clustered inference and the bootstrap and multiplicity procedures reported in the article (notebooks 05 and 08). Weekday/weekend contrasts, station-level estimates, the alternative MIMA imputation, hourly profiles, season-stratified contrasts and leave-one-zone-out analyses are used as diagnostics and sensitivity checks (notebooks 02, 03, 04, 09 and 10, and the corresponding sections of 05). The day-level contrasts of notebook 06 are the between-day comparison reported alongside the primary analysis in the article. The interrupted-time-series, regression-discontinuity-in-time and GEE analyses of notebook 07 are secondary exploratory analyses; they are kept for completeness and reproducibility, are not reported in the article and are not used to define its headline conclusion. Each notebook states its role in this hierarchy in its header.

Data
----
All air-quality measurements originate from **SIMA** (Sistema Integral de Monitoreo Ambiental), the official hourly monitoring network of the Monterrey Metropolitan Area, Nuevo León, Mexico (2023-01-01 to 2025-12-31, five pollutants: CO, NO2, O3, PM10, PM2.5). The network comprises fifteen physical stations named by urban sector; per SIMA, each station's representative monitoring radius is roughly 2–3 km. The analysis uses the complete official network: stations of the same sector family are consolidated into six monitoring zones (an empirical coherence check supports the pooling), and a station-level disaggregation is analyzed separately.

**Data access and terms.** The hourly measurements are the property of SIMA (Gobierno del Estado de Nuevo León). SIMA provided the annual workbooks to the authors for this study on the condition that they are used in the analysis but not redistributed. This repository therefore contains **no measurement series at any resolution**: neither the annual workbooks (`data_raw/`, git-ignored) nor the consolidated hourly, station, zone, day-window and gap files that notebooks 00 and 01 build from them (also git-ignored). What the repository does contain is the complete code, the official SEP school calendar, the trained MIMA model and every derived result reported in the article (estimates, diagnostics, aggregated curves, tables and figures), so that each number in the article can be traced to the notebook and cell that produced it.

To rerun the analysis, request the hourly data from SIMA (Sistema Integral de Monitoreo Ambiental, Agencia de la Calidad del Aire de Nuevo León, https://aire.nl.gob.mx/), place the three annual workbooks in `data_raw/` as `BD 2023.xlsx`, `BD 2024.xlsx` and `BD 2025.xlsx` (one sheet per station named with its SIMA code, first column `Fecha y hora` or `date`, one column per parameter) and run the notebooks in numeric order. Every notebook checks for its inputs and stops with a clear bilingual message if they are absent. Any other network with hourly station data can be analysed with the same code by reproducing the long format that notebook 00 builds (station, zone, timestamp, parameter, value).

- Built locally by notebook 00 from the workbooks (not distributed): `base_horaria_estaciones_fisicas.csv.gz` (hourly series per physical station), `base_horaria_zonas.csv.gz` (hourly series by monitoring zone), `datos_horarios_imputados_hibrido.csv.gz` (hybrid imputation) and, through `data_processed/mima/mima_pipeline.py`, `datos_horarios_imputados_MIMA.csv.gz` (MIMA imputation; `--reuse-model` applies the included trained model in minutes, without the flag it retrains in about two CPU hours).
- Built locally by notebook 01 (not distributed): `panel_pico_hibrido.csv`, `panel_pico_MIMA.csv` (day–zone panels for the 07:00–09:00 arrival window) and `gaps_franjas_hibrido.csv`, `gaps_franjas_MIMA.csv` (within-day log-gaps against the 10:00–12:00 control window).
- Included: `data_processed/calendario_oficial_sep.csv`, `inicios_bloques_clase.csv`, `cortes_placebo.csv` (the official SEP calendar and the dates derived from it, public information); `censo_datos/` (coverage and missing-data counts, station → zone mapping, intra-zone coherence; no measurements); `resultados_metodos/`, `analisis_estacional/`, `estaciones_fisicas/`, `perfil_horario/`, `graficos_metodos/` (estimates, diagnostics, aggregated diurnal curves and figures); `mima/` (script, trained model, audit metrics and comparison figures).

Notebooks
---------
- `Models/00_datos_desde_cero.ipynb`: full pipeline from the SIMA workbooks — parsing, station → zone consolidation with an intra-zone coherence check, the missing-data tables (by zone and by physical monitor: consolidation cuts the missing share from 11.2% of monitor-hours to 2.1% of zone-hours), the hybrid imputation (implemented here) and the MIMA alternative, and the construction of the official SEP school calendar from the DOF acuerdos. Its header states the data terms and the exact workbook format expected in `data_raw/`.
- `Models/01_calendario_y_franjas.ipynb`: defines and justifies the two intraday windows (arrival 07:00–09:00; same-day control 10:00–12:00), builds the window aggregates from the imputed hourly series, and constructs the analysis panels and peak/off-peak gaps with the official calendar as the school-term indicator.
- `Models/02_censo_datos.ipynb`: data census — consolidation robustness, hourly coverage per zone/pollutant/month, fully-imputed cells in the arrival window, and day counts per season × school regime × weekday/weekend (balance checks; the article's Table 1).
- `Models/03_analisis_estacional.ipynb`: season-stratified analysis — monthly seasonal pattern by regime (exploratory boxplots), class-vs-vacation contrasts inside each season (with month fixed effects), season-vs-regime magnitude comparison, and the window design by season.
- `Models/04_auditoria_MIMA.ipynb`: audit of the MIMA imputation (artificial-gap metrics, physics/chemistry checks) and hybrid-vs-MIMA comparison.
- `Models/05_did_franjas.ipynb`: the primary analysis — within-day double difference (arrival vs control window), with formal hypotheses and assumptions, weekday/weekend triple difference, the calendar-based falsification test (alternating ISO weeks inside each regime, reported with coefficients and intervals), a leave-one-zone-out influence analysis, the same estimator without month fixed effects (to show how much of the raw within-day widening of NO2, O3 and PM2.5 is seasonal), and residual diagnostics (Durbin–Watson and the autocorrelation function of the residuals up to 21 lags, levels vs gaps).
- `Models/06_sur_conjunto.ipynb`: joint SUR with Wald tests on day-level concentrations (simple difference), unadjusted vs adjusted specification; each joint test computed with the conventional (independent-error) covariance and with the weekly-clustered covariance to measure what temporal dependence costs; PC1; falsification checks and leave-one-station-out; validated against statsmodels.
- `Models/07_its_rdit.ipynb`: secondary exploratory analyses, not used in the article — interrupted time series (ARMA errors by AIC), per-cut jumps, regression discontinuity in time with donut and reference cuts, GEE AR(1).
- `Models/08_inferencia_fina.ipynb`: the primary inference — wild cluster bootstrap, TOST equivalence tests against 10% and 20% reference effect sizes, one-sided 95% upper confidence limits, Romano–Wolf correction.
- `Models/09_perfil_horario.ipynb`: full diurnal profiles (school vs vacation weekdays), the hour-by-hour coefficient with controls (hours without school activity as reference windows), station-level heterogeneity, and PM levels vs WHO 2021 annual guidelines.
- `Models/10_estaciones_fisicas.ipynb`: the window design disaggregated to each of the fifteen physical stations (observed hours only), with a pooled fixed-effects check (the article's Fig. 2, with the station names mapped to SIMA sector codes).

Methods and Techniques
----------------------
- Reproducible data pipeline from the SIMA hourly records to the analysis panels (the records are obtained from SIMA; every notebook documents and checks its inputs).
- Official SEP school calendar (DOF acuerdos for the 2022–23 to 2025–26 school years) as the school-term indicator, with a four-category day type (weekday/weekend × school/vacation).
- Two independent imputations (hybrid seasonal-profile, implemented in notebook 00, and the MIMA deep-learning model); every analysis is run under both.
- Explicit hypotheses and assumptions per method. Primary: the within-day double difference (the collapsed two-window form of a stacked specification with date×zone fixed effects) with weekly cluster-robust inference. Reported alongside it: joint SUR Wald tests on day-level contrasts with conventional vs weekly cluster-robust covariances. Secondary exploratory: ITS, RDiT, GEE.
- Season-stratified contrasts with month fixed effects inside each stratum; station-level disaggregation.
- Publication-grade inference: null-imposed Rademacher wild cluster bootstrap (B = 999), TOST equivalence tests against reference effect sizes, one-sided 95% upper confidence limits, Romano–Wolf stepdown correction; Durbin–Watson and residual-ACF diagnostics.

Key Findings and Conclusions
---------------------------
- **Within the day, the school calendar is associated with a wider morning CO gap.** The 07:00–09:00 arrival window exceeds the same-day 10:00–12:00 control window by 4.6% more on school-term days than on vacation days (95% CI [1.5, 7.7]; joint Wald p = 2.1e-4; wild cluster bootstrap p = 0.005; Romano–Wolf adjusted p = 0.021). No other pollutant shows an individual effect.
- **The effect is bounded.** The one-sided 95% upper confidence limit for the CO percentage effect is 7.2%; equivalence tests rule out school-term effects of 10% or more for CO, NO2, PM10 and PM2.5 and of 20% or more for all five pollutants. These bounds support the main estimate; they are not the headline.
- **It has the shape of morning school traffic.** It is concentrated on weekdays (+5.5%, p = 0.003) and absent on weekends (+1.4%), the hour-by-hour coefficient peaks inside the arrival window, and the CO estimate is positive at all fifteen physical stations (pooled +4.7%, p = 0.011); it is largest in spring (+7.2%).
- **At the level of full days there is nothing detectable once season and weekday are controlled.** Direct comparisons of the 07:00–09:00 window between school-term and vacation dates give a joint Wald p = 0.31 with weekly-clustered errors (the unadjusted contrast, p = 0.025, inherits the seasonal cycle). The same tests with a conventional independent-error covariance would report p < 1e-41 and p < 1e-9: that gap is the cost of ignoring temporal dependence.
- **Robustness.** Falsification checks with an artificial calendar are clean within school terms (p = 0.506) and borderline within vacations (p = 0.046, reported as a caution); dropping each zone in turn leaves the CO estimate between 3.9% and 5.1% with every interval excluding zero; the individual student-return dates show no consistent jump; and the two imputations give the same conclusions (CO +4.4% under MIMA).
- **Interpretation.** The regressor is the school calendar, which moves correlated morning mobility along with the school run, so the finding is reported as a "school-term morning association" rather than an effect of school trips alone. In exposure terms, children arrive at school exactly when the morning peak forms, and a single-digit, bounded, recurring widening of the CO peak is a policy-visible target for staggered entry times, collective school transport and anti-idling enforcement.

How to Run
----------
Python 3.11 with pandas, numpy, matplotlib, seaborn, scipy, statsmodels (plus `python-calamine` to read the SIMA workbooks in notebook 00, and torch only for `data_processed/mima/mima_pipeline.py`). First obtain the SIMA workbooks and place them in `data_raw/` (see Data access and terms). Then run notebook 00, run `python mima_pipeline.py --reuse-model` inside `data_processed/mima/` to produce the MIMA-imputed series with the included trained model (or without the flag to retrain, about two hours on CPU), and run notebooks 01 to 10 in numeric order; 01 builds the panels every later notebook uses. Runtime is a few minutes per notebook (08 runs 4 bootstrap blocks of 999 replications). The notebooks are distributed with their executed outputs, so every result can be inspected without the data.
